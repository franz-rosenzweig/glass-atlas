# Medicines

The patient takes ten medicines, and a lot of what matters about them is how they get along: which ones need to be hours apart, which ones pull the heart rate in opposite directions, which ones add up. The medicines part of the atlas is built around that, with a page for each medicine, a planner for the whole day, and a bench of its own for Hadlima, the injection.

![The medicines list, searched for "as needed", with Zofran open](images/medicines.png)
*The list searched for "as needed", and Zofran open beside it, with the heart lit for the medicines that lengthen the QT interval together.*

## The list

Press **Medicines (10)** at the bottom of the case, and the left panel turns into the list: Propranolol XR, Lexapro, Levothyroxine, Adderall XR, Low-dose naltrexone, Hydroxychloroquine, Hadlima, Zofran, Tizanidine and Ibuprofen, each with what kind of medicine it is. **Back to the patient's chart** takes you back to the conditions.

The "Search medicines" box filters the list as you type, by the name or by the kind, so "SSRI", "beta blocker" or "as needed" all work. **Escape** clears it.

## One medicine

Click a medicine and the drawer opens with three tabs. **Instrument** is the planner, with that medicine picked out and the body lit where it acts. **Dive** zooms from the body down to where the medicine works, cells and molecules included (see [The dives](dives.md#the-medicine-dives)). **Read** says what it does in plain words, with a red **For any provider** box where something matters in an emergency, the condition it treats (click it to go there), and the sources. A medicine also shows up as a link at the bottom of the condition it treats.

## The planner

![The planner](images/planner.png)
*The patient's day, hour by hour: the meals along the top, a dot for each medicine, the as-needed ones to tick, and the checks underneath.*

The first row of the list, **The patient's day, hour by hour**, opens the planner for all of them together. It's a 24-hour chart: the meals along the top, and a dot for each medicine at the time it's taken. Drag the dots to try a different schedule, and the checks underneath update as you go, in green when a timing fits the label's advice and in red or amber when it doesn't. For example, levothyroxine wants an empty stomach 30 to 60 minutes before food, iron and levothyroxine need four hours apart, and hydroxychloroquine goes with a meal.

The three as-needed medicines (Zofran, Tizanidine and Ibuprofen) are off until you tick them for "today", and then they get a row and their own checks.

Under the checks, "Where the patient's medicines overlap" has a button for each way they add up: **Timing**, **Heart rhythm (QT)**, **Blood pressure and pulse**, **Seizure threshold**, **Pain relief in an emergency**, **Stomach and absorption** and **Serotonin**. Each one lights up the part of the body involved, picks out the medicines that share it, and says in a paragraph what to watch for.

The times are a starting template, not a real schedule, and the planner isn't a dosing plan. The spacing rules come from the medicines' labels, and the sources are on the **Read** tab.

## Hadlima

![The Hadlima bench](images/hadlima.png)
*Hadlima's bench: the level in the blood over twelve weeks, and the injection sites on the real skin.*

Hadlima is adalimumab-bwwd, a biosimilar of Humira for the patient's RA, injected every other week. A medicine taken every two weeks doesn't fit a 24-hour planner, so clicking it opens a bench of its own instead.

**The level in the blood** is a chart of twelve weeks, built from the numbers on its label: a peak about five and a half days after each dose and a half-life of about two weeks. Pick **Every other week**, **Weekly** or **One dose late**, and switch on **With methotrexate** (it clears more slowly) or **Antibodies to the drug** (it clears faster). The lowest and highest levels and when it reaches a steady state update as you go, with a paragraph on what each one means and where the numbers come from.

**Where it goes** puts the injection sites on the body itself: the lower belly and the front of the thighs, with the circle around the belly button that's off limits. **Next dose** walks through a rotation of eight spots, with this dose in orange and the last three fading, and **Belly**, **Right thigh** and **Left thigh** move the view. Under it are the rules from the instructions, from taking it out of the fridge first to the sharps container.

**Call the same day** has the signs that need a call, like a fever, signs of TB, an allergic reaction or new numbness, each with what to do. And **With the patient's other conditions** has a button for each place it meets the rest of her case, from infections and vaccines to the cervix, pregnancy and surgery, each lighting up the part of the body involved, with its sources.

The **Read** tab is the long version: what a biosimilar is, how it works, the trials, how it's taken and stored, the checks before starting, vaccines, the rarer warnings, surgery, and pregnancy. The **Dive** tab follows one dose from the injection to the molecule, Hadlima beside Humira.

The model is illustrative, and nothing here is a dosing plan.
